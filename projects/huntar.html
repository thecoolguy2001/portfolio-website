HuntAR

A modern AR reimagining of the classic light-gun hunting game - ducks fly through your real room, and you aim your phone like a lightgun to bring them down.

Active Development

Role

Founder / Game Designer / iOS Developer

Timeline

August 2026 - Present

Team

Demonte Walker

Tools

RealityKit, ARKit, SwiftUI

Problem

Classic light-gun games like Duck Hunt never made the jump to modern hardware - most "remakes" are flat touchscreen ports that lose the physical act of aiming at something.

Solution

An ARKit and RealityKit-powered hunting game that places ducks directly in the player's real environment, turning the phone itself into the lightgun.

August 2026

Solution

HuntAR uses RealityKit to scan the player's surroundings and populate real, physical space with ducks - flying through the living room, over the kitchen counter, past whatever's actually in frame. The phone becomes the gun: move it to aim, tap to fire.

Real-Space Ducks

RealityKit anchors ducks to the player's actual room instead of a flat, fixed background.

Phone-as-Lightgun

Aim by physically moving the phone, not swiping a touchscreen - a reticle marks where the shot will land.

Score & Streak

A star counter tracks hits, rewarding consecutive shots to drive replay.

Nostalgic Core, Modern Shell

The classic Duck Hunt formula, rebuilt for a phone camera instead of a CRT and a lightgun peripheral.

August 2026

Core Game Loop

The loop has to work in any room, with no setup beyond opening the app and pointing the camera around.

01

Scan Space

ARKit maps the player's surroundings so ducks have real surfaces and depth to fly through.

02

Ducks Spawn

Ducks fly into the scanned environment on paths that use the room's actual geometry.

03

Aim & Shoot

The player physically moves the phone to line up the reticle, then taps to fire.

04

Score

Hits add to the star count; misses break the streak.

August 2026

Problem

Duck Hunt's lightgun era hasn't really been recreated since the CRT and peripheral it needed went away. Most modern "remakes" swap the gun for a touchscreen tap, which loses the part that made the original fun - physically aiming at something.

I wanted to bring that physical aiming back using hardware everyone already carries: the phone's camera and ARKit's ability to place objects in real space.
