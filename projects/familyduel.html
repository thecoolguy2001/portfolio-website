April 2026

Core Loop

Two teams — Family A and Family B — sit around the TV. Each round, a survey-style question appears on screen; whichever team buzzes in first from their phone gets first crack at naming a top answer. Guess wrong and the other team can steal the points by matching one of the remaining answers.

Phone as buzzer

Every player's iPhone connects over the local network and doubles as a low-latency buzzer — no extra hardware required.

Survey-style questions

Answers are ranked by popularity rather than binary right/wrong, rewarding teams that can predict what most people would say.

Steal mechanic

A wrong guess hands the remaining points to the other team if they can land one more matching answer — keeping both teams engaged every round.

TV as the shared screen

The Apple TV owns the score, timer, and reveal animations so the whole room is watching the same thing at once.

April 2026

Phones as Controllers

A single Siri Remote can't serve two competing teams, so Family Duel routes input through a lightweight companion layer instead — every player's phone joins the game as its own buzzer over Multipeer Connectivity, with the Apple TV acting as host.

No app install friction

Joining a game is built to feel like joining a Wi-Fi network, not downloading and configuring a separate app before play can start.

One buzzer, one job

The phone screen during a round shows nothing but a big buzz button — all the game state lives on the TV where everyone can see it.

April 2026

Design Decisions

Everything on the TV side is designed for a couch, not a desk — legible from across the room, with almost nothing that needs to be read up close.

10-foot UI

Oversized type, high contrast, and generous spacing throughout — every screen is designed to be read from a couch, not a desk.

Team color, not player color

Every visual cue ties back to Team A or Team B rather than individual players, keeping the on-screen state simple even as players join or drop out mid-game.

Host controls stay off-screen

Game setup and question packs are managed from a phone before the round starts, keeping the TV screen free of menus once play begins.

April 2026

Problem

Apple TV has almost no local multiplayer party games built for a room full of people. The single Siri Remote makes head-to-head competition awkward, and there's no built-in way to give two teams their own buzzer without extra hardware.

Project Started

Family Duel began as an attempt to bring a proper local multiplayer party game to the living room — two teams, phones as buzzers, and the TV running the show. Core buzz-in and pairing came together first; the question bank and presentation polish are ongoing.
