Pod

A Flipper-to-Flipper encounter game over sub-GHz radio — cross paths to log encounters, then battle in a live 10-second tap race. No add-on hardware required.

Problem

Flipper Zero peer-to-peer interaction usually needs active input; there's no automatic way to “encounter” other owners.

Solution

Broadcasts a callsign over sub-GHz radio, logs encounters with nearby Flippers, and starts a live tap-race battle.

July 29, 2025

Results

Pod is teaching me how to turn background radio scanning into interactive storytelling — and how to build a wireless protocol I could trust before two devices ever met: the wire format is host-tested for round-tripping and CRC rejection.

Real two-device battles are implemented and coded to the protocol; the live round-trip is being shaken out with a second Flipper. Until then, Practice mode lets anyone play the tap race against a simulated opponent.

Process

Creating a passive multiplayer experience using the Flipper's wireless capabilities.

01

Concept Research

Studied passive multiplayer and proximity-based encounter systems for inspiration.

02

Protocol Design

Designed self-framed radio messages — a 1-byte type, CRC-8, and de-duplication — for beacons and the challenge/accept/tap exchange.

03

Battle Mechanics

Designed the live duel: both players opt in, a 3-2-1 countdown fires, then it’s a 10-second OK-mashing tap race — most taps wins and earns XP.

04

Host-Tested Protocol

Validated the wire format on the host — round-tripping and CRC rejection — and built Practice mode so the battle game is playable with a single Flipper.

July 12, 2025

Visual Design Direction

Pod leans into retro handheld nostalgia — pixel-art glyphs, chunky type, and the Flipper’s own dolphin as your identity. The visual language signals "game" the moment a Flipper boots into it.

Your dolphin is your fighter

Your real dolphin level seeds your beacon and battles — but Pod never touches the actual pet. Game progress lives in its own save on the SD card.

The tap race is theater

A 3-2-1 countdown, ten frantic seconds of OK-mashing, then the verdict. The battle is physical — whoever taps fastest wins.

Walk Mode is a radar

Leave it open as you move around — it broadcasts your calling card every few seconds and lists who’s in range. Press OK to challenge.

Ranks worth chasing

Wins and XP climb a ladder of sea creatures — Minnow, Guppy, Tuna, Dolphin, Orca, Kraken — persisted on the SD card between sessions.

June 26, 2025

Solution

Pod uses the Flipper’s built-in sub-GHz radio — no add-on hardware. While Walk Mode is open, it broadcasts a tiny calling card (your callsign, a random ID, and your dolphin level) on 433.92 MHz every few seconds, and listens for others.

No Extra Hardware

Runs on the Flipper’s built-in sub-GHz radio at 433.92 MHz — you just need two Flippers to play for real.

Passive Encounters

Catching someone’s beacon logs the encounter automatically — with battle results, XP, and "seen X ago."

Opt-In Tap Battles

One player challenges, the other accepts — then it’s a live 10-second tap race for XP and rank.

June 5, 2025

Problem

The Flipper Zero community thrives on interaction and creativity, but most peer-to-peer activity requires active input or hacking modules. There's no playful, automatic way for Flipper owners to recognize and battle each other in the wild.

The challenge was creating a social gaming experience using only hardware every Flipper already has — the built-in sub-GHz radio.

June 2, 2025

Project Started

Pod is a Flipper Zero app that turns passive encounters into live battles, bringing a nostalgic, retro-gaming twist to real-world interactions.

Built on the Flipper’s built-in sub-GHz radio, it quietly broadcasts your dolphin, logs everyone you cross paths with, and lets two players who linger near each other battle it out — best at meetups, cons, and hackerspaces where Flippers gather.
