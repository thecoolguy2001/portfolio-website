September 2026

The Concept

An endless driving game where the road never resolves into a finish line and the other cars on it are real people. The pitch is closer to a shared cruise than a race: you drive, other players appear alongside you, and the run continues for as long as you want to keep going.

Endless road

No laps and no finish line. The road keeps generating ahead of the player so a session can last one minute or twenty.

Shared with real players

Other drivers on the road are other people, appearing and dropping away as they join and leave, instead of scripted traffic.

Drop in, drop out

No lobbies or countdowns. Opening the app should put a player on the road within seconds.

One thumb

Controls need to work one handed for a game meant to be opened in short bursts. The exact input scheme is still open.

September 2026

Open Questions

The concept is clear enough to argue with, which is the useful part of ideation. These are the decisions that change what the game is, so they get settled before anything gets built.

What are players doing together?

Racing, cooperating, or just sharing space. An endless road with no finish line means the reason to care about other cars has to come from somewhere else.

Do cars collide?

Solid cars make other players matter but invite griefing. Ghost cars are safer and lower stakes but risk making multiplayer feel decorative.

How does a run end?

Endless games still need a reason to stop. Crashing, running out of fuel, or simply leaving are all different games.

How many players per road?

A handful of cars feels personal; a crowd feels alive but raises netcode cost and visual noise on a phone screen.

September 2026

Problem

Mobile driving games tend to sit at two extremes. Endless runners are easy to pick up but lonely, and racing sims are social but demand a real time commitment before anything happens. There is room between them for something that opens fast, asks for one thumb, and still puts real people on the road.
