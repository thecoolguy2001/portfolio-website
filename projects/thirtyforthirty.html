Challenge Complete — 30/30

Thirty projects shipped. Not every idea survived contact with a one-day deadline — two builds (pop and fakedeep) failed and were scrapped mid-month — but scrapped ideas didn't count. They got replaced, and thirty still had to ship.

June 30, 2026

What I Learned

Shipping daily taught me more than any single large project — and it completely changed my sense of what a browser tab can do.

The Browser Is Wildly Underrated

In one month I shipped local LLMs, AR, real physics, computer vision, and multi-device play — and only one project of the thirty needed a server.

Anything Can Be a Controller

Breath, body pose, phone tilt, browser zoom, window size, even CPU load — the platform is full of inputs nobody uses. Finding them became the game.

Ruthless Scoping

One day per build means deciding fast what a project is not. Cutting scope became a skill, not a compromise.

Ideas Are Allowed to Fail

Two builds got scrapped mid-month. Killing them quickly and replacing them was cheaper than rescuing them — a lesson that only a hard deadline teaches.

June 2026

The Rules

For the challenge to actually make me a better developer, it needed constraints that couldn't be gamed.

01

One Project Per Day

Scoped, designed, built, and shipped in a day. No rollovers, no banking days ahead.

02

Something New Every Day

Each build had to explore a technology or technique the others didn't — no two days exercising the same muscles.

03

The Browser Does the Work

No servers unless the idea truly demanded one (exactly one did). Everything else runs by opening an index.html.

04

Failed Ideas Don't Count

An idea that isn't working gets scrapped and replaced, not rescued — but thirty finished projects still have to ship.

June 2026

Why #30for30

Tutorials and long-term projects only exercise a narrow slice of web development. A big project settles into one stack and one set of patterns; a tutorial hands you the decisions already made.

I wanted a forcing function that would push me through the breadth of web technologies — and make me a faster, more confident developer by building constantly instead of occasionally. Thirty small, complete projects would touch more of the platform in one month than one large project touches in a year.

June 1, 2026
