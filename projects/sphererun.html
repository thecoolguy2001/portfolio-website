July 2026

Core Loop

SphereRun is a one-thumb game — no thumb required at all, in fact. Tilt the phone to steer a rolling sphere left and right down a narrow track that keeps scrolling forward and speeding up. Miss a jump or clip an obstacle and the run ends; boost orbs along the way reward tight lines with a temporary speed burst.

Tilt steering

CoreMotion maps device tilt directly to lateral sphere movement, smoothed specifically for one-handed, on-the-go play.

Procedural track generator

Course segments — straightaways, gaps, obstacle clusters, boost lanes — are assembled at runtime so no two runs repeat.

Escalating speed

The track accelerates the longer a run survives, so the difficulty curve is built into time-on-track rather than hand-placed levels.

GameKit leaderboards

Best-distance leaderboards give runs something to chase beyond a single session's high score.

July 2026

Design Decisions

One mechanic, tuned hard, beats several half-tuned ones — the whole design budget for SphereRun went into making tilt steering and speed ramp feel right.

Tilt only, no taps

Jumping happens automatically at marked gaps rather than on a tap — keeping both hands free and the input surface to a single axis.

Smoothing over raw sensitivity

Raw CoreMotion input reads twitchy. A short smoothing window on tilt input was the single biggest feel improvement in two weeks of tuning.

Speed as the difficulty knob

Rather than hand-authoring harder levels, the track generator stays constant and global scroll speed ramps over time — simpler to build, and it reads as "the game is testing you," not "the game changed."

Runs stay short

Most runs are designed to end within a minute or two. A quick loss-to-retry loop matters more for a tilt runner than a long single session.

July 2026

Problem

One-thumb, tilt-only games are easy to pitch and hard to make feel good. Most either steer too twitchy to trust or too sluggish to feel exciting once the track speeds up — and I wanted to see how far a single, well-tuned mechanic could carry a whole game.

July 2026

Project Started

SphereRun began as a two-week scope: a single mechanic — tilt-steer a rolling sphere down an ever-accelerating course — pushed as far as it could go in that window. Core loop, controls, and track generation came together fast; the remaining time is going toward tuning and store prep.
